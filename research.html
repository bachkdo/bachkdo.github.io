Research Overview

Bayesian Optimization and Surrogate Modeling

I develop Bayesian optimization methods for expensive engineering problems where each simulation or experiment can be costly. My interests include Gaussian process modeling, Thompson sampling, acquisition-function design, and practical inner-loop optimization strategies that remain accurate while scaling to more difficult search spaces.

A recurring theme in this work is balancing theoretical rigor with engineering usability. I am interested in algorithms that do not just benchmark well in toy settings, but also transfer to real design problems with constraints, uncertainty, and multimodal objectives.

Multi-Fidelity Learning and Optimization

Many engineering workflows naturally produce information at multiple fidelity levels: simplified models, coarse meshes, reduced-order surrogates, and high-fidelity simulations. I study ways to combine these sources effectively so that optimization and inference become faster without sacrificing reliability.

This includes both reviewing the current state of multi-fidelity Bayesian optimization and designing practical methods for when low-fidelity information is informative but imperfect.

Uncertainty Quantification, Reliability, and Robust Design

Another part of my work focuses on uncertainty-aware engineering decisions. I am interested in reliability-based design optimization, robust design, and probabilistic methods that account for uncertain loads, materials, model discrepancy, and data limitations.

These problems often require careful coupling between simulation, statistics, and optimization. I aim to build methods that reduce computational cost while preserving the decision relevance of uncertainty estimates.

Inverse Problems in Solid and Structural Mechanics

I also work on inverse problems arising in mechanics, including parameter identification and data-assisted constitutive modeling. This line of work connects optimization with physical modeling, especially when the forward model is nonlinear, expensive, and only partially observed through measurements.

More broadly, I am motivated by methods that let us infer hidden material or structural information in a way that is both computationally feasible and physically meaningful.

AI for Engineering Design

I am interested in the growing overlap between optimization, uncertainty quantification, and modern machine learning for engineering design. This includes scientific machine learning, data-efficient generative methods, and design pipelines that embed physical understanding rather than treating engineering problems as generic black-box tasks.

In practice, I care about methods that help engineers search design spaces more intelligently, learn from limited data, and make better decisions under uncertainty.
